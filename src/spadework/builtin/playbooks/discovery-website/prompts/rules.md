## House style for a website discovery pack

- The pack is read by the person who will quote and build the website, and
  parts of it are shown to the client. A non-technical client should be able
  to follow every recommendation, and a developer should be able to act on
  it without asking what you meant.
- Every recommendation gives its reason, and the reason points to something
  in this project: a priority, a constraint or a fact in the brief, an input,
  or another document in the pack. Refer to the brief as "the brief", and to
  inputs and pack documents by filename.
- Use the client's own words for their products, services and customers, as
  the brief and inputs write them. Call the same thing by the same name
  throughout; pages and content types take the names given in
  04-information-architecture.md.
- Each topic has one home: software and running costs in 06-tech-stack.md,
  the page list and content model in 04-information-architecture.md, content
  production in 07-content-plan.md, legal pages in 08-seo.md, and dates in
  09-roadmap.md. Elsewhere, refer to that document by filename instead of
  repeating it.
- Money: every figure has a currency, says whether it is one-off or recurring
  (per month or per year), and says whether tax is included when the source
  says so. Estimates are ranges, with their basis in a few words.
- Effort is a range of person-days, never hours and never a single number.
- Do not recommend anything 01-scope.md rules out, and do not add features
  nobody asked for. If you think the scope misses something important, raise
  it as an open question instead.
- Open questions are written for the client: plain words, one question each,
  answerable in a line (yes or no, a choice, a number, or something they
  already have, such as a list or a file).
- The site languages (the languages the website is published in) are not the
  language this pack is written in. Keep the two apart.
