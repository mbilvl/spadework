This document says who the website is for and what each of them needs to see
before they act. The designer, the copywriter and the developer all work from
it, and 03-references.md and 05-design-direction.md build on it directly.

Treat the key facts from 01-scope.md (goals, what is in scope, languages) as
settled. Take everything you say about audiences from the brief and the
inputs. This task has no web research: where knowing more about an audience
would change a recommendation, ask for it as an open question rather than
filling the gap with a generic persona.

Write these sections as `##` headings, in this order, with exactly these names:

- **Audiences**: a table of two to five audiences, most important to the
  business first: who they are, what brings them to the site, what they want
  to do there, and how much the business depends on them. Use the groups the
  brief and inputs describe. An audience you infer is labelled as an
  inference.
- **What each audience needs**: for each audience, the questions the site must
  answer for them and the one action we want them to take.
- **Trust signals**: the concrete proof this client can show (for example
  certifications, standards met, years trading, types of customer, finished
  projects, facilities). Mark each one "confirmed" if the brief or an input
  states it, or "to confirm" if it is only likely. Do not credit the client
  with proof they have not claimed.
- **Positioning**: one or two sentences on how the client should come across
  compared with the alternatives their audiences consider, then three to five
  messages that follow from it, each backed by something in the brief or
  inputs.
- **Languages and tone**: which languages each audience needs and which
  content to translate first, then the voice, as a short "do / don't" list.
  Use the site languages settled in 01-scope.md; if they are not settled,
  say what each option means for each audience.
- **Open questions**

Check the audiences against the brief's priorities: if a priority implies an
audience the brief does not describe, raise it as an open question.

Keep the document under 150 lines.
