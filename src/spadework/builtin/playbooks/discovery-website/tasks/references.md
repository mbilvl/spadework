This document shows the client and the designer what good looks like for a
site like this one, and what the competition does. It is the evidence behind
choices in 04-information-architecture.md and 05-design-direction.md, so each
observation must end in something this project can use or avoid.

The research notes staged with you are the only source for facts about other
websites. Use only the sites they cover; do not add sites from memory. The
research read page text, not visuals, so describe structure and content, not
looks.

Judge every reference from the point of view of the audiences and needs in
02-audience.md: say which audience a pattern serves and why it matters to
them.

Write these sections as `##` headings, in this order, with exactly these names:

- **Regional peers**: a table with one row per peer: who they are, what the
  site does well, and what it does badly, for this project's audiences.
- **Best-in-class references**: the same for each standard-setting site, plus
  the one feature on it most worth studying.
- **Patterns to adopt**: five to eight patterns, ranked by how much they help
  the brief's priorities. For each: where it was seen, which audience it
  serves, and why it fits this client.
- **Patterns to avoid**: three to five, each with why.
- **Open questions**: for example, which of these sites the client likes or
  dislikes, and which sites their own customers use.

If the research found fewer peers or references than it was asked for, show
the ones it found, say how many are missing, and record the shortfall as a
gap. Do not pad a table with weak or unsourced examples.

Keep the document under 200 lines.
